**Gleanings and Questions from Our Experiences of Housing, What Housing Does, and Sense of Being “At Home”**

**Cost of Housing and Implications for Costs of Living**

- Housing is expensive\!
- When housing takes a large percentage of our monthly income, less remains for other living expenses: food; heating and cooling and other utilities; transportation; family care; education and professional development; etc.
- The social safety net is inadequate to cover the gaps, especially for those who are not eligible (immigrant status, eligibility thresholds)
- Security deposits (and first and last month rent and other fees ) add to the costs.
- Expensive housing leads to crowding.
- If we don’t have money to live alone, we have to cohabitate.
- Living with others can limit personal space and privacy, especially if they are not our friends and family.
- The lack of smaller units in the housing stock exacerbates both the cost of available housing and the need to cohabitate.
- Housing is especially expensive in many of the neighborhoods we most want to live: amenities; safety; proximity to work, family and community, and other places we frequent.
- Ability to contend with the costs of housing is tied to social-economic _class._
- Intergenerational wealth allows greater access to housing, including for those with lower personal or household income.
- Where you live can signal class/wealth/income status to others.

- What are the advantages of being able to choose the community where one lives? What does _choice_ mean to us? (c.f. how housing costs limit our options).
- One example is families’ decision to move to neighborhoods with schools and educational opportunities they prefer.

- What are the desired outcomes and what are their underlying theories of such programs?
- For whom are they most and least effective? And in what ways?
- What are the advantages and disadvantages diverse communities (or buildings) \-- with respect to income and/or other background, experience, or identity (e.g. race, ethnicity, political/ideological perspectives)?
- What are the desired outcomes and what are their underlying theories of programs/policies that aim to cultivate mixed-income or other diversity?
- For whom are they most and least effective? And in what ways?

- How do we build a system with less emissions, less car dependency

- What are the distinctive needs for rural areas and other areas farther from the urban core?
- How can we call attention to these needs and better serve rural and suburban communities?

- Housing experiences are “conditional” \-- One often needs permission to be in a place and that others control who may be there and how they interact.

- What does it mean to exist as an unhoused person in a place/community?
- How does physical form influence political form.
- How can we recognize and address power dynamics present in communities? Who controls the agenda? Who has power? Who decides?

- What resources are impacted by the ownership of land?

**Sense of “Being At Home”**

**We feel “at home” when….**

- When we move towards abundance.
- What happens to people without inter-generational wealth, especially in a system that is changing?
- How does the tenant body influence power? Being an educated tenant matters.
- How does market power affect the power of “consumers”? Supply scarcity feeds landlords’ power. So does the state that is embedded and shapes how the market operates.
